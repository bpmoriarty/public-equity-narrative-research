# Morningstar, Inc. (MORN) — Discussion points, FY2021–FY2025

Patterns across the window, the questions they raise, and where stated priorities and paid-for priorities diverge.

## Observations

### The measurement basis changed in every year of the window, and almost always without restatement

Across FY2021–FY2025 Morningstar repeatedly redefined, reclassified or withdrew the metrics investors use to track it, and in most cases explicitly declined to restate prior periods. In FY2021 prior-period AUM/AUA was revised to reclassify CIT assets, and the Advisor Workstation client count was revised to include Canada [SEG-FY2021-f7f2b9ad, SEG-FY2021-f3e45f62]. In FY2022 management stopped disclosing Advisor Workstation client counts altogether, on the stated ground that the metric was "not as relevant a metric and not highly correlated to revenue given the heterogeneity of our client contracts," and moved premium website subscriptions to annual reporting — both disclosed in written investor answers filed under Regulation FD [QA-FY2022-dcd6c8ec, QA-FY2022-313f3eb1]. Starting Q1 2023 revenue-type classifications were updated with "Prior periods have not been restated" [EVT-FY2023-9f7bdca8]. In FY2024 PitchBook media sales revenue moved from license-based to transaction-based, again without restatement [SEG-FY2024-be5495df]; LCD ceased to be reported separately [QA-FY2024-99db828a]; and the definition of compensation expense was widened to include commissions, severance and stock-based compensation, with prior periods retroactively adjusted [LANG-FY2024-84cb08c4]. In FY2025 the annual renewal rate methodology was revised to include mid-contract contract-value changes [EVT-FY2025-bc8998b2], the segment was renamed Morningstar Direct Platform with its key product areas recomposed [SEG-FY2025-7671a5d5], and the PitchBook licensed-user definition was refined, under Regulation FD, to focus on third-party licensed users alongside a rationalisation of the internal user count [QA-FY2025-087470db].

Two of these disclosures also contain admissions of error: exchange market data was omitted from the 2023 renewal-rate calculation "due to an oversight in our calculation" [QA-FY2025-8f2aa792], and adjusted diluted EPS for Q2 2023 was corrected following the identification of "a clerical error in the calculation" [QA-FY2023-1d4e7aca]. No single filing shows this frequency. The pattern does not establish intent, but it does mean that almost none of the operating metrics in this window are cleanly comparable end to end, and that the direction of travel in several — Advisor Workstation clients, Direct licenses, PitchBook licensed users — is obscured at precisely the points where the disclosed figures were softening.

### The non-core label entered the vocabulary in 2024 and has since functioned as a leading indicator of exit

The word does not appear in the FY2021–FY2023 material in this pack. It arrives with the Commodity and Energy Data sale, where management said it "unlocked value for a non-core business" [QA-FY2024-b946fd5c]. By FY2025 it had become an explicit governing test: "if we come to the conclusion that an activity is non-core, we may exit or deemphasize those activities" [QA-FY2025-1d72a177] — both Regulation FD disclosures. In the same year Direct Web Services was described parenthetically as "(non-core)" when explaining anticipated churn [QA-FY2025-fd089218], and exchange market data was labelled "a non-core product" with the lowest retention among Data products [QA-FY2025-8547296c], having already been shifted to being managed "for profitability rather than revenue growth" [QA-FY2025-28f70f19].

The realised track record behind the label is unambiguous: Commodity and Energy Data sold for $52.4 million [EVT-FY2024-7c7ac24d], US TAMP customer assets sold to AssetMark [EVT-FY2024-88a80322], Morningstar Office slated for retirement [SEG-FY2025-b7e05709], and second-party opinions to be retired by end-Q1 2026 [SP-FY2025-6552c93b]. Exchange market data is the one product currently carrying the label that has not been exited or wound down.

### PitchBook's total addressable market grew roughly fivefold while the test for inclusion in it was loosened

Management's own TAM series, disclosed under Regulation FD, runs $2.3 billion (2020) to $3.5 billion (2021) to $4.9 billion (2022) to $6.5 billion (April 2023) to $11.1 billion (2025) [QA-FY2021-476f24fc, QA-FY2022-4dcf2ebf, QA-FY2023-44d8ce16, QA-FY2025-e7d32a9d]. In 2021 and 2022 the methodology required a firm to be active in at least one supported use case, and three use cases for the companies segment [QA-FY2022-4dcf2ebf]; management defended the figure pre-emptively, arguing the estimate captured clients that were "ready" rather than a "wish list" [QA-FY2021-31c3cc9d]. By FY2025 the definition had been changed in 2024 to include corporates with a single use case, and corporates account for roughly 60% of TAM logos [QA-FY2025-8958d245].

That is the same segment management has described, year after year, as its weakest: "our most vulnerable segment in the current market environment" [QA-FY2023-2a54a3c6]; the segment where it "reevaluated sales and customer support strategies for corporates with limited use cases" [QA-FY2024-3958446f]; and, by late 2025, the segment where "corporates with a market intelligence use case are less sticky and some are choosing competitors over PitchBook" [QA-FY2025-800d6304]. The TAM expansion and the churn concentration point at the same population.

### Pricing was used hard as a growth lever, produced measurable client loss in the flagship platform, and was then quietly normalised while the disclosed metric was reframed

In December 2022 management said it was "generally implementing higher-than-typical annual price increases for 2023," ranging from mid-single digits to low double digits [QA-FY2022-a09cd0bf]. In June 2024 it confirmed the consequence directly: "we instituted a meaningful price increase for Morningstar Direct licenses. With the increase, some large asset managers (especially in the U.S.) reduced their licenses" [QA-FY2024-7bcafb9b]. The same answer pivots the framing — "Direct license counts do not tell the full story of how clients access our data and research" [QA-FY2024-edd97044]. By September 2025 the retreat is explicit: "Since mid-2024, price increases have been more in line with prior years. We do not comment on future price increases" [QA-FY2025-14ff3c22]. The FY2025 shareholder letter then reports that Direct "license growth has been flat," with growth instead coming from revenue per license and reporting-solutions expansion [LANG-FY2025-687b635c]. All the pricing disclosures are Regulation FD material; only the outcome appears in the letter.

### Margin improvement rests in part on a labour-geography shift the filings present only obliquely

In September 2023 management attributed falling adjusted operating expense per employee "in part [to] the increased proportion of our employee base located in India, where our labor costs are lower than in many other markets" [QA-FY2023-3c954b66] — a Regulation FD answer. The FY2022 China restructuring shifted global business-function work to Mumbai, Toronto, Madrid and Chicago [EVT-FY2022-618af085], and management confirmed the India headcount increase was "due in part to the initiation of the reduction and shift of our China operations" [QA-FY2022-2f069ace]. By the FY2024 10-K, approximately 41% of employees worked in India against 30% in the US [HC-FY2024-e74fc8c0]. Over the same period headcount fell from 12,224 to 10,973 [HC-FY2022-b5b67998, HC-FY2025-eaacb74b].

The FY2024 letter presents the efficiency gain in a different vocabulary: "Average revenue per employee increased to roughly $200,000 from $170,000 in 2023" [LANG-FY2024-de81b210]. Nothing in the letter connects that figure to geographic mix. Management also flagged India as its highest-turnover market [QA-FY2022-4508154b] and, when asked about business-continuity concentration risk, described a general programme without an India-specific contingency [QA-FY2024-ac8c1933].

### The justification for depressed margins kept its structure across the whole window while rotating its subject

FY2021: "we are currently in a period of extraordinary investment because our opportunity set is meaningful" [QA-FY2021-0c90d456]. FY2022: investments "can put pressure on margins in the short-term, but we do not anticipate that they will come at the expense of long-term returns or future margin expansion," with Wealth, ESG/Sustainalytics and PitchBook named [QA-FY2022-c17c4233]. FY2023: margins "close to an annual low point in our investment cycle" [QA-FY2023-30864c4f]. FY2024: growth expected to be "largely self-funded" [QA-FY2024-9b7edc51]. FY2025, on AI: returns "may not always materialize in the first year" [QA-FY2025-f2b05587]. The subject changed — ESG, then Wealth, then AI — but the shape of the claim (near-term dilution, long-term return, no date attached) did not. All Regulation FD.

### Self-description migrated from incumbent-leader to challenger to disruptor — and then the company bought scale in the business it calls a disruptor

In FY2021 management described DBRS Morningstar as having been highly successful in expanding its footprint in the smaller, private and transactional segments where all registered agencies can compete, while acknowledging the safeguards the three large legacy agencies enjoy elsewhere, and set out a list of differentiators headed by "Largest Independent Analyst Research Staff" [QA-FY2021-28bc1e9d, QA-FY2021-6358f5c1]. By FY2024 the framing was inverted: Morningstar Indexes as "a highly flexible challenger brand" [QA-FY2024-8c656f83], Credit as one of the areas "where we are challengers to the incumbents" [QA-FY2024-575dc63d]. FY2025 escalates: solutions that "credibly challenge traditional providers" [QA-FY2025-d1289e64], and "We have positioned Morningstar Indexes as a disruptor in the index space for a number of years and intend to continue to play this role," with value-based pricing for core index capabilities [QA-FY2025-07f734bb]. In the same period the company agreed to acquire CRSP, which management says "will position us as one of the largest index providers for public US equity index funds" [QA-FY2025-5c7c819e], for approximately $365.0 million against $55 million of annual revenue [EVT-FY2025-49025db0, QA-FY2025-ed6e3050]. Disruptor pricing and scale ownership are not incompatible, but the filings do not reconcile them.

### Shareholder support has been cited each year as the reason for leaving pay design alone; the most recent vote breaks the pattern

The CD&A repeats a near-identical formula in every year of the window: significant shareholder support, therefore no changes made in response to the say-on-pay vote [LANG-FY2021-cf48e888, LANG-FY2022-20b1e3a4, LANG-FY2023-57d45987, LANG-FY2024-d93d15c2, LANG-FY2025-3802d594]. The underlying counts, per the Item 5.07 filings: 507,847 against at the meeting held 13 May 2022; 521,906 at 12 May 2023; 346,649 at 10 May 2024; 547,563 at the 9 May 2025 meeting; and 1,206,815 in the vote reported 8 May 2026 on fiscal 2025 compensation [VOTE-FY2021-ded0d9e1, VOTE-FY2022-9a876b5d, VOTE-FY2023-4a38447e, VOTE-FY2024-117d729d, VOTE-FY2025-0400d889, EVT-FY2022-42dca9a4, EVT-FY2023-05068e23, EVT-FY2024-d44361e4, EVT-FY2025-7410dc50]. Opposition roughly doubled to the highest level in the window in the year the CD&A itself discloses one-year TSR of -35.1% [LANG-FY2025-c6ee1d35], the CEO's salary rose 23.8% [LANG-FY2025-df503ab2], and stretch PSUs continued to be granted with no grant-date fair value recorded [LANG-FY2025-a4405c1e]. Director dissent moved in the opposite direction over the same period: Steve Joynt drew 9,935,476 against at the 10 May 2024 meeting, 550,723 at the 9 May 2025 meeting, and 279,943 in the vote reported 8 May 2026 [VOTE-FY2023-76c7ae52, VOTE-FY2024-6b1263f6, VOTE-FY2025-2b6f95a2], while Bill Lyons's against votes rose across the same three meetings from 2,068,504 to 2,597,678 to 2,434,521 [VOTE-FY2023-c5cb3374, VOTE-FY2024-74373328, VOTE-FY2025-0cd57cef].

## Open questions

**What is exchange market data's revenue, and is it held for disposal?** It is the only product currently labelled "non-core" that has not been exited [QA-FY2025-8547296c], it is being run "for profitability rather than revenue growth" with revenue declining [QA-FY2025-28f70f19], it had the lowest retention in Morningstar Data between 2022 and 2024 [QA-FY2025-8547296c], and it was the item accidentally excluded from the 2023 renewal-rate calculation [QA-FY2025-8f2aa792]. Every other product given that label has since been sold or retired.

**What was PitchBook's 2025 TAM on the pre-2024 definition?** The inclusion test for corporates was loosened from three use cases to one [QA-FY2022-4dcf2ebf, QA-FY2025-8958d245], corporates are roughly 60% of TAM logos [QA-FY2025-8958d245], and corporates are the segment with the highest churn where management has deliberately deprioritised prospects with limited use cases [QA-FY2025-800d6304, QA-FY2024-3958446f]. How much of the $11.1 billion is in a cohort management is actively choosing not to sell to?

**What is the three-year AOI goal underlying the stretch PSUs, and how far above the annual budget does it sit?** The awards vest nothing at 100% of the target AOI goal, 100% at 112.5% and 200% at 125% [INC-FY2024-13fe47c0, INC-FY2025-a6cf01d2], and are described as "designed to be extremely challenging," with no grant-date fair value recorded because attainment was not deemed probable [LANG-FY2025-a4405c1e]. Yet AOI came in at 111.1% of the annual target in 2024 and 111.3% in 2025 [INC-FY2024-a958d46f, INC-FY2025-0be6b354]. If the three-year goal is built off annual budgets set on a similar basis, the "extremely challenging" threshold sits roughly where the company has landed twice running — and a potentially large award is invisible in the Summary Compensation Table.

**Which product deliveries were delayed, and what did the reduction cost?** The FY2025 CD&A states that "due to a few delayed product deliveries, our CEO recommended and the Compensation Committee approved the application of negative discretion to reduce the 2025 annual incentive payout to 111.4% of target" from a formulaic 115.5% [LANG-FY2025-ffbd19db]. The products are not named. The Regulation FD record separately discloses that Direct Advisory Suite delivery slipped and "negatively impacted renewals and new client acquisition" [QA-FY2025-287717ee]. Is that the same event, and were there others?

**Has the Compensation Committee ever exercised its stated discretion to limit the bonus on sharing-ratio grounds?** Management describes monitoring "the 'sharing ratio' expressed as a projected bonus payout as a percentage of AOI each quarter," with Committee discretion to limit the bonus if it is misaligned [QA-FY2025-e40e1959], and concedes that at target "we expect the bonus pool to grow faster than profit" in percentage terms [QA-FY2025-d3a13409]. What is the ratio, and has it ever triggered?

**What has unallocated corporate expense actually done as a percentage of revenue since 2023?** Management has committed to "reducing these costs as a percentage of revenue over time" in at least three separate Regulation FD responses across FY2024 and FY2025 [QA-FY2024-bc6dc038, QA-FY2025-73170d03, QA-FY2025-18f582d9]. Because Sustainalytics and Indexes are reported inside the same Corporate and All Other line [SEG-FY2025-48c19c34], an outside reader cannot separate overhead discipline from the performance of those two businesses. Who is accountable for that line, and what is the trend excluding the two operating segments inside it?

**Did the LCD contingent consideration pay out in full, and what is LCD revenue today?** The $50.0 million contingent payment was structured to pay "based on the percentage of customer ACV that has been successfully unbundled as of December 1, 2022" [QA-FY2022-ce121a7e], and the full $50.0 million was paid on 6 February 2023 [EVT-FY2023-eee82e8f]. Yet management later described "a small amount of expected customer leakage" and said LCD "did not contribute meaningfully to overall PitchBook segment growth in 2023" [QA-FY2024-fdf43d88, QA-FY2024-0d1af78a], before folding LCD reporting into PitchBook entirely [QA-FY2024-99db828a]. Full payment on an unbundling-linked earnout alongside a non-meaningful growth contribution is worth reconciling.

**Was declining to build a CLO ratings business a decision or an outcome?** Management concedes it does "not have a significant business in rating collateralized loan obligations, while our largest competitors are more active in this sector, which has helped drive their growth" [QA-FY2024-c00f5b66], and repeats in FY2025 that competitor ABS issuance tracks the leveraged loan market where "We do not have significant exposure to CLO ratings" [QA-FY2025-9a100b02]. Over the same period Credit repositioned CMBS staff after becoming "more pessimistic on the likely speed of the recovery in CMBS" [QA-FY2023-354eddf3] and grew private credit from slightly under a fifth to roughly a quarter of transaction-based revenue [QA-FY2025-ba39a686]. Was CLO entry evaluated and rejected, and on what return threshold?

## What management appears to prioritise

**From incentive metrics — what actually pays.** The paid-for priority is profit, and increasingly so. Annual bonus weighting moved from 67% Adjusted Revenue / 33% Adjusted EBITDA in FY2021–FY2022 [INC-FY2021-0a208533, INC-FY2022-42de7490] to 50/50 in FY2023 [INC-FY2023-15467d28], then swapped EBITDA for Adjusted Operating Income in FY2024 and extended the equal weighting to the Chief Revenue Officer, removing his revenue tilt [INC-FY2024-a958d46f, LANG-FY2024-cfef5211]. The 60% funding floor was removed for FY2025 [LANG-FY2025-5471b5fe]. Two further details matter more than the weightings. First, in both FY2024 and FY2025 revenue landed at or barely above target (99.4%, 100.5%) while profit beat it by roughly eleven points (111.1%, 111.3%) [INC-FY2024-d9219b43, INC-FY2024-a958d46f, INC-FY2025-ca7261d8, INC-FY2025-0be6b354] — the profit line, not the revenue line, is where the plan has been paying. Second, the only multi-year revenue target visible in the pack failed outright: the 2022 MSU revenue kicker required a 15.5% three-year Adjusted Revenue CAGR and achieved 9.8%, so no modifier applied [INC-FY2025-69a6d8dc], against the 2021 grants' 12.0% versus an 11.2% target [INC-FY2024-a1be3fa9]. On the equity side, 75% of the annual award is market stock units keyed to absolute three-year TSR with a 20% target and a 200% maximum [INC-FY2023-c6107240, INC-FY2025-5e3b5247].

**From repeated language — what management emphasises in its own voice.** Shareholder letters are present for FY2022 through FY2025 and absent for FY2021, so this is a four-year, not five-year, record. Three things recur. Candour as a device: "There's no sugarcoating" [LANG-FY2022-72430c42], "The investments were sizable and poorly timed" [LANG-FY2023-9fb8d5ec], "self-inflicted wounds" [LANG-FY2023-47723c3a], "We weren't getting to scale fast enough" [LANG-FY2024-5628ad58]. Moat vocabulary turned inward — the letters describe an internal business as "the moatiest part of our business" [LANG-FY2024-0040d02a] and argue "our moat is not a single asset or technology. It is a compounding system in which data fuels research" [LANG-FY2025-a56ba2a4]. And capital-allocation distinctiveness: "We don't buy stock to offset dilution like others do" [LANG-FY2022-5a2dbcb0]. Outside the letters, two boilerplate passages are identical in all five 10-Ks — the no-forecasts philosophy [LANG-FY2021-f24fa9c3, LANG-FY2022-00971bc9, LANG-FY2023-9f9f571c, LANG-FY2024-c1fe3662, LANG-FY2025-43da23ef] and the closing claim that the business "shown resilience and flexibility navigating global macroeconomic trends" [LANG-FY2022-20e686ec, LANG-FY2023-fb2fc98f, LANG-FY2024-6a3ff1f9, LANG-FY2025-1f786257].

**From capital allocation — where the money went.** 2021 was a year of no divestitures at all [EVT-FY2021-03624f99] and a $400.0 million buyback authorisation of which only 4,900 shares, $1.3 million, were used [EVT-FY2021-65ebc07e]. Deployment then went into acquisition: LCD at up to $650.0 million [EVT-FY2022-3ba1e0d2], Praemium's UK and international business [EVT-FY2022-8e66ef1e], the ¥8 billion MJKK brand-licence termination [EVT-FY2023-820f47c1]. The successor $500.0 million buyback was similarly dormant — 8,484 shares for $1.4 million by end-2022 [EVT-FY2022-5d620550], 33,300 shares for $11.6 million in 2024 [EVT-FY2024-75b980fa] — until $487.0 million was spent through 28 October 2025, completing the programme [QA-FY2025-4e43615b], with a new $1.0 billion authorisation approved 29 October 2025 [EVT-FY2025-0a50e037]. The dividend, by contrast, rose every year: 36c quarterly guided for 2022, 37.5c for 2023, 40.5c for 2024, 45.5c for 2025, 50.0c for 2026 [EVT-FY2021-5a723e00, EVT-FY2022-173d9869, EVT-FY2023-af8e5e3f, EVT-FY2024-067b7b1c, EVT-FY2025-c7866b89] — subject throughout to credit-agreement covenants restricting dividends [EVT-FY2022-0465763b, EVT-FY2025-74e6df24]. The revealed preference over four of five years is unambiguous: acquisitions and a steadily rising dividend ahead of repurchase, with repurchase treated as a late-cycle, valuation-triggered action.

### Where stated and paid-for priorities diverge

**ESG was a headline strategic priority for two years and no metric ever paid for it.** "Establish a leading ESG position across each business" was a named Item 1 priority in FY2021 and FY2022 [SP-FY2021-bcbe7c18, SP-FY2022-4f07b4fe]. No incentive metric in any year of the window references ESG, Sustainalytics, emissions or any non-financial measure, and management confirmed this directly under Regulation FD: "Presently, we do not tie executive remuneration to our emissions milestones" [QA-FY2023-1866ef1e]. The innocent reading is available from the pack — Sustainalytics revenue flowed into Adjusted Revenue, which carried 67% weight during exactly those two years. But that is also the point: the metric in force during the ESG build rewarded the top line while Sustainalytics was, on management's own account, the lowest-margin licence business and "a drag on our overall margins" [QA-FY2022-925ce9e3, QA-FY2022-45445308]. The pay design tolerated the margin dilution that the company subsequently spent three years reversing.

**Talent and culture is stated every year and paid for nowhere.** Building an inclusive culture with exceptional talent appears as a strategic priority in FY2021, FY2022, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 [SP-FY2021-45276d8d, SP-FY2022-53e476da, SP-FY2023-55f4566c, SP-FY2024-7a0f0111, SP-FY2025-50eb8e38]. No metric attaches. The available innocent explanation is the discretionary individual performance factor — 140% for the CEO in FY2021, 100% in FY2023, 120% in FY2024, 110% in FY2025 [INC-FY2021-0271a21f, INC-FY2023-5e6704b0, INC-FY2024-d8854e1f, INC-FY2025-9ee4e21c] — which could in principle capture people outcomes. But the disclosed rationales for those factors cite cost discipline, financial results and product delivery, not talent measures [LANG-FY2023-ef3486eb, LANG-FY2025-ffbd19db]. Over the same period the disclosed engagement score fell from 80% to 69% to 64% [LANG-FY2023-551fb62a, QA-FY2025-9c0c811b] with no visible consequence in the pay outcome.

**AI is now the lead strategic priority and carries no metric.** "Leverage advances in AI" entered the 10-K priorities in FY2023 [SP-FY2023-d263222a] and was elevated in FY2025 to "Lead in AI to Empower Investor Success," described as making Morningstar AI-native [SP-FY2025-2672bc89]. Nothing in the incentive metrics references it. Here the innocent explanation is strong and comes from management itself: AI returns "may not always materialize in the first year" [QA-FY2025-f2b05587], and the three-year stretch PSU construct on AOI [INC-FY2025-a6cf01d2] is the natural place for such returns to land eventually. Management also distinguishes companywide goals from executive metrics, having added AI adoption as a companywide goal in 2024 [QA-FY2024-2727498a]. The divergence is real but explicable.

**In the other direction: absolute TSR carries three-quarters of equity pay and appears in no stated priority.** Market stock units on absolute three-year TSR are 75% of the target annual equity mix [INC-FY2023-c6107240, INC-FY2025-5e3b5247], yet no strategic priority in any year of the window mentions shareholder return, share price or total return. Because the measure is absolute rather than peer-relative, its outcomes have been erratic relative to operating performance: 2018 grants paid the 150% maximum on TSR of 140.6% and 156.8% [INC-FY2021-747a36e0]; 2019 grants paid 165.2% [INC-FY2022-91344f34]; and the FY2022 CD&A notes TSR "far exceeded the target goals" in a year the same document discusses a -36.3% one-year return [LANG-FY2022-95441327]. The defensible reading is that absolute TSR is the cleanest alignment measure and the hurdle was raised from 10% to 20% partly on shareholder feedback [QA-FY2025-3d72d9bb]; the analyst reading is that the single largest pay component is untethered from anything management has told shareholders it is trying to do.

**Where the two do converge.** Operational excellence and scalability is stated in all five years — as "Drive operational excellence and scalability to support growth targets" through FY2024 and as "Scale for Impact" in FY2025 [SP-FY2021-018bc28a, SP-FY2022-ca001ef1, SP-FY2023-ba0a9b36, SP-FY2024-9652582b, SP-FY2025-0acc5ca6] — and it is the one priority whose paid-for weight increased across the window: EBITDA at 33% in FY2021–FY2022, AOI at 50% from FY2024, plus three-year AOI stretch PSUs [INC-FY2021-10693dc6, INC-FY2024-a958d46f, INC-FY2024-13fe47c0]. The alignment is genuine, but it dates from FY2023, not from the start of the window. Similarly, the growth priority — differentiated insights across public and private markets, stated FY2021 through FY2024 [SP-FY2021-1ea36587, SP-FY2022-001645b2, SP-FY2023-3558bf34, SP-FY2024-9454d09b] — did have one long-horizon paid metric in the MSU revenue kicker, and it is the only stated priority in the pack that is both measurable and demonstrably missed [INC-FY2025-69a6d8dc].
